I.

About the Society

Moot Court Society at Pitt is an AMCA affiliate chapter where members research, brief, and argue a real appellate case each year. It's the same one competing schools across the country are arguing at regional tournaments.

Every member trains across three tracks at once: the substantive law behind the year's case, oral advocacy in front of a bench, and written brief construction. You don't need prior debate or mock trial experience. You need to be willing to get cross-examined by a judge and come back the next week having fixed your argument.

II.

First GBM

Wednesday, September 23 · 9:00 PM
David Lawrence Hall, Room 106

Come find out what moot court actually is, meet the officer team, and get the case packet for tryouts. No experience or background in law required. Just show up.

What you'll be trained in

  • Legal research and case analysis: reading a record, spotting the live issues, and building an argument on top of real doctrine.
  • Oral advocacy: arguing in front of a bench, thinking on your feet, and holding your position under hostile questioning.
  • Persuasive brief writing: structuring and citing a written argument the way an actual appellate brief is built.
  • Cross-examination and judicial questioning: learning to answer, not deflect, when a judge tries to find the hole in your argument.

Training and feedback come directly from Pitt Law School faculty, current Pitt Law students, and pre-law professors, not just peer critique.

III.

This Year's Case

Bobby Bronner v. United States
Fall 2026 AMCA competition case

Our team is arguing a case at the intersection of Fourth Amendment search doctrine and executive power. It's the kind of dispute that shows up in real Article II litigation, not a hypothetical built for the classroom.

  • Issue I, Fourth Amendment: whether long-term, warrantless pole camera surveillance of a residence constitutes a search requiring a warrant.
  • Issue II, Article II: the scope of executive authority to authorize that surveillance without judicial or legislative check.
IV.

How the Program Works

Members train across three parallel tracks all semester, converging on the same case from three directions.

Track one

GBM Curriculum

General body meetings walk through the record, the circuit split, and the doctrine underneath both issues, building the shared knowledge every advocate needs before stepping up to argue.

Track two

Oral Advocacy

Structured argument practice in front of a mock bench, with rounds of questioning designed to expose the soft spots in your position before a real judge finds them first.

Track three

Brief Writing

Workshops on issue framing, record citation, and persuasive structure, building toward a competition-ready brief on both the Fourth Amendment and Article II questions.

VI.

Try Out

Argue your way onto the team

Tryouts are open to all Pitt students. No moot court, debate, or pre-law background required. You'll get the case materials in advance, argue in front of our officer bench, and get direct feedback either way.

The Eastern Regional caps competing teams at four per school, so our tryout bench is competitive by design. Bring your best argument, not your most polished résumé.
